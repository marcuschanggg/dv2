Western Australia · January–December 2025

Twelve months of birds in Western Australia

Nearly 38,500 sightings, submitted by birdwatchers across Western Australia, tell a story of who's common, who's rare, and how activity shifts with the seasons.

02 · Effort

Where the birds were counted

Sightings have been pooled into ~15km cells. More points mark wetlands and reserves where large flocks gather. Alongside the map, a look at which kinds of survey produced the records, and a calendar of when they came in.

Total individual count per cell.

Shore and backyard surveys make up the vast majority of records.

Each square is one day of 2025 (darker means more records); the bars give the average for each weekday. Records peak in October–November and dip in August, and Sundays are busiest (11.5 a day, against 7.0 on Tuesdays). Based on the 3,414 records in this extract.

05 · Species

Six species to watch

Some WA birds are homebodies; others range widely. The Western Rosella and Red-capped Parrot, both WA endemics, stay tightly confined to the South West, while the Rainbow Bee-eater ranges much further north.
